Quick verdict

  • Lean toward Jobber if you want a widely used contractor CRM with strong quoting/scheduling fundamentals and a large user community across home services.
  • Lean toward Housecall Pro if you want a marketing-heavy platform with sales tools, phone/pipeline features (plan-dependent), and an affiliate/partner ecosystem that often markets to exterior cleaning niches.
  • Neither is “best for everyone.” Solo operators may overbuy; multi-truck shops should prioritize dispatch clarity, mobile apps for techs, and payment speed.

What matters for pressure washing ops

  1. Fast quotes: Driveways, houses, roofs, and fleets need templated line items and optional photos — both platforms support estimates; how you configure price books matters more than brand.
  2. Weather-driven reschedules: You need easy drag-and-drop rescheduling and customer notifications. Test this in a trial before you commit.
  3. Route / day packing: Multi-stop soft wash or commercial routes benefit from clear map/list views. Confirm what your plan actually includes.
  4. Deposit + balance collection: Exterior jobs often take deposits. Compare payment links, card fees, and offline/cash recording.
  5. Crew permissions: Techs should see job notes and checklists without full admin access.

Pros & cons

Jobber — strengths

  • Mature product for quoting, jobs, and invoicing
  • Large contractor user base and training content ecosystem
  • Clear fit for service businesses that live in estimates → jobs → invoices

Jobber — watch-outs

  • Advanced marketing/sales features may require higher tiers or add-ons
  • Payment processing fees reduce margin — model them into job pricing
  • Migration from spreadsheets still takes setup time

Housecall Pro — strengths

  • Strong marketing of growth tools (calls, campaigns, reviews — plan-dependent)
  • Visible partner/affiliate programs aimed at home services niches
  • All-in-one positioning can reduce tool sprawl for growing shops

Housecall Pro — watch-outs

  • Higher-tier pricing can climb quickly — match features to actual needs
  • Sales/phone features may be overkill for a solo washer
  • Same caveat: card fees and contract terms need a careful read

Who each is for

  • Solo / 1–2 truck pressure washing: Either can work. Prioritize simple quoting, SMS reminders, and mobile invoices. Don’t pay for enterprise marketing you won’t use.
  • Growing multi-crew soft wash / exterior shop: Score both on dispatch clarity, role permissions, and reporting. Run parallel trials with real jobs.
  • Shops already using one: Switching costs are real (customers, price books, habits). Switch only if a concrete workflow is broken.

FAQ

Can I use either platform just for pressure washing?

Yes. Both are general field-service tools used across trades. You’ll customize services, price lists, and checklists for wash packages.

Do I need a CRM if I’m still on paper and Venmo?

If missed follow-ups or unpaid invoices cost you jobs, a CRM usually pays for itself. If you’re truly occasional side-work, a simple invoice tool may be enough until demand grows.
