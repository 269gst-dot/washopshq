How to Price Pressure Washing Jobs (Worksheet Framework)

Updated September 2026 · Pricing ops guide · Affiliate disclosure

Pricing pressure washing and soft wash work is less about copying a stranger’s “$0.XX per sq ft” and more about covering your real costs, paying yourself, and leaving margin for weather, callbacks, and growth. This page is a worksheet-style framework — not a list of invented rates we claim to have tested in your market. Plug in your numbers; verify local competition and insurance requirements yourself.

1) Job cost floor worksheet

Before you pick a customer price, know the floor: the minimum that covers direct costs and a fair labor rate. Fill blanks with your actuals.

Example math only (not a recommended rate): If your floor is $200 and you want 40% gross margin on sell price, sell ≈ $200 ÷ 0.60 ≈ $333. Replace every figure with yours.

2) Package pricing framework

Most exterior cleaning shops sell packages, then adjust. Build a price book with clear inclusions/exclusions.

Package (example names) Scope questions to lock Pricing method options
Driveway / concrete Sq ft or stalls? Oil stains extra? Sealing separate? Flat by size band, or $/sq ft with min ticket
House wash (soft wash) Stories, sq ft, roof included?, gutters?, windows? Size bands (S/M/L) + story adder
Roof soft wash Pitch, access, material, plant protection time Often separate line; higher risk → higher floor
Fleet / commercial Unit count, frequency, water access, after-hours Per unit + mobilization; contract discounts for volume
Add-ons Gutters, sidewalks, patios, rust/oxidation, dumpster pads Fixed adders in your CRM price book
  1. List every surface you will (and won’t) touch.
  2. Write a one-line “includes / excludes” for each package.
  3. Set a minimum ticket so tiny jobs still cover mobilization.
  4. Store packages in your CRM so quotes stay consistent across estimators.

4) Quote workflow tips

  • Measure once, template forever: Photos + rough dimensions beat guessing from the curb when stakes are high.
  • Separate “restore” vs “maintain”: First clean often costs more than annual touch-ups — say so on the estimate.
  • Deposits on big jobs: Especially roofs and commercial — spell out cancellation terms.
  • Don’t race to the bottom: If a lead only wants the cheapest bid, your floor worksheet tells you when to walk.
  • Review monthly: Fuel, bleach, and insurance move. Revisit floors and package bands on a schedule.

FAQ

Should I price by square foot or by package?

Many shops use size bands or packages for speed, and fall back to measured $/sq ft (with a minimum) for odd properties. Pick one primary method so quotes stay consistent.

How do I handle “can you beat this quote?” calls?

Ask what’s included. Match scope first. If scope matches and you’re still above your floor, decide consciously — don’t automatically cut below your worksheet.

Do you publish a national rate chart?

No. National averages hide local labor, insurance, and competition. This framework is intentional: your costs → your floor → your packages.
